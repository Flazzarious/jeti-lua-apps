## Feature description (paste into `/speckit-specify`)

Flameout Alarm is an in-flight telemetry app for the Jeti DS-24 II that warns
the pilot, by sound and on screen, when the turbine appears to have flamed out.

**Problem.** A turbine flameout in flight is easy to miss. The model keeps
flying, the engine noise is already distant, and the pilot may not notice the
loss of thrust until the model is slow, low or out of glide range. Many ECUs
send RPM over telemetry, so the transmitter can tell the moment the engine
spools down while the pilot still expects it to be running.

**Goal.** Give an immediate, unmistakable alarm when the RPM falls below a
critical threshold while the engine is supposed to be running, and stay
silent during normal start-up, idle, throttle chops and intentional shutdown.

**Why an app and not a native alarm.** Jeti's native telemetry alarms can fire
on "RPM below X", optionally gated by a switch. But they can't tell "the
engine has not started yet" from "the engine was running and stopped", so a
low-RPM alarm also sounds during the whole start sequence and after every
shutdown. The app adds arming: it only alarms after the engine has
demonstrably been running at idle or above.

**Per-model enable and RPM sensor assignment.** Not every turbine sends an
RPM telemetry sensor. The app is therefore configured per model:

- Monitoring is **off by default** on every model.
- The user turns it on for a model only if that model's turbine provides an
  RPM sensor, and assigns that sensor from the model's telemetry sensors
  (`system.getSensors()`).
- Monitoring cannot be turned on until an RPM sensor is assigned. If the list
  has no sensors, the app explains that the turbine/ECU in this model must
  send RPM telemetry and that telemetry must have been received at least once
  (transmitter and receiver powered on).
- When monitoring is off or no sensor is assigned, the app is fully inactive:
  no sounds, no warnings, and its telemetry window shows "OFF".
- The enable flag and sensor assignment are saved per model
  (`system.pSave` / `system.pLoad`), so each model keeps its own setting.

**Startup and auto-restart RPM.** During a start, and during an ECU
auto-restart after a flameout, the starter and ignition spin the engine
through a range of RPM below idle. That startup range must never arm the
app and must never clear an active flameout alarm. Only reaching idle RPM
(or above) counts as "engine running".

**Alarm sound (approved).** The alarm repeats a 5-second cycle until it is
acknowledged, cleared by a relight, or stopped by the Cut switch:

1. **Voice callout:** "Flameout! Flameout! Flameout!" spoken quickly and
   imperatively (about 2 s), in the same Piper "Amy" voice that Speed Gauge
   uses for all its speech (Speed Gauge spec, User Story 6 and
   FR-030–FR-037), so all AG- apps sound consistent.
2. **Lock tone:** a rapid, high-pitched beeping like a missile-lock warning
   fills the rest of the cycle.

A new callout starts every 5 seconds (callout start to callout start), so
the pilot hears the voice again, then the beeping resumes.

Both sounds are audio files in the app's own `AG-FlmOt/` folder, not
references to Speed Gauge's files, so the install stays standalone. The
voice file follows Speed Gauge's voice rules (FR-034–FR-036):

- Generated by the repo's voice script (`tools/voice/`) from the same Piper
  Amy model. The script's phrase list gains "Flameout!" with output to
  `AG-FlmOt/`.
- Delivered urgently, unlike Speed Gauge's normal-paced callouts: Piper
  length scale 0.70 (about 30% faster), noise scale 0.5, pitch raised about
  1 semitone without changing speed (ffmpeg `asetrate` ×1.07 +
  `atempo` 0.9346), high-pass at 150 Hz, +4 dB presence boost at 3 kHz and
  light compression (threshold −18 dB, ratio 4, attack 3 ms, release
  60 ms, makeup 4 dB) so it cuts through wind and engine noise. Repeats are
  separated by 0.12 s.
- Mono, 16-bit, 16 or 22.05 kHz; leading/trailing silence trimmed;
  normalized to the same peak loudness as Speed Gauge's files.
- Not committed to git (`.gitignore`); each developer generates it locally.
  A credits note for Piper (MIT) and the Amy model (CC BY-SA 4.0) goes in
  the folder and in `CREDITS.md`.

The lock tone is a plain synthesized tone (no voice licensing), so it can be
committed or generated by the same script.

Approved samples (2026-10-02, kept in the claude.ai "LUA Apps" project chat,
not committed): lock tone `AG-FlmOt_lock_loop.wav` (seamless 2 s loop,
1800 Hz with a 3600 Hz overtone, 45 ms on / 35 ms off with 3 ms fades,
12.5 beeps/s, 22.05 kHz mono 16-bit); urgent voice
`AG-FlmOt_flameout_x3.wav`; and the full 20 s demo
`AG-FlmOt_alarm_demo.wav` (4 cycles). The voice samples were made with
Piper Amy **low** (16 kHz); the release file should use the same Amy model
quality as Speed Gauge, with the settings above.

**Setup inputs.**

- Enable monitoring for this model (off / on). On only if an RPM sensor is
  assigned.
- RPM telemetry sensor for this model.
- Throttle safety switch and its Cut (OFF) position. Same 2-position switch
  used by the Turbine Throttle Setup app, but selected independently here.
- Idle RPM (the arming threshold). Entered by hand or captured with a
  "learn idle" helper (record the steady idle RPM with the engine running).
  It must sit at or above idle and above any RPM the engine reaches during
  start-up or auto-restart.
- Flameout threshold in RPM, below idle. Suggested as a set percentage of the
  idle RPM (editable), low enough that a throttle chop to idle doesn't cross
  it.
- Arming time: how long RPM must stay at or above idle before the app arms,
  or before an alarm clears after a relight.
- Detection delay: how long RPM must stay below the flameout threshold before
  alarming, to ride out telemetry glitches.
- Optional: ECU status sensor, if the ECU reports one.

**Behavior.**

0. **Off.** When monitoring is off for the model or no RPM sensor is
   assigned, the app does nothing at flight time except show "OFF" in its
   telemetry window.
1. **Disarmed** at model load when monitoring is on. No alarm in this state.
   Startup-range RPM (below idle) keeps it Disarmed.
2. **Arming.** When the switch is not in Cut and RPM stays at or above the
   idle RPM for the arming time, the app moves to **Armed** and plays a short
   "armed" confirmation (optional).
3. **Flameout.** While Armed, if RPM stays below the flameout threshold for
   the detection delay and the switch is not in Cut, the app raises the
   alarm: the 5-second cycle of "Flameout!" ×3 then lock tone, repeating,
   plus stick vibration if available and a large on-screen "FLAMEOUT" in its
   telemetry window.
4. **Auto-restart.** While in Flameout, RPM rising through the startup range
   (starter cranking, ignition, fuel ramp) does not clear the alarm, and
   wobbles in that range never re-trigger it as a new event. The alarm
   clears only when RPM is back at or above idle for the arming time; the
   app then returns to Armed and can announce "relit" (optional). If the
   restart fails, the flameout state persists.
5. **Intentional shutdown.** Moving the switch to Cut disarms immediately and
   silently, so a normal shutdown never alarms.
6. **Acknowledge.** The pilot can silence the alarm with a key press. The
   app stays in an acknowledged Flameout state (still shown on screen) until
   RPM is at or above idle for the arming time, or the switch goes to Cut.
   It does not re-alarm for the same event.
7. **Telemetry lost.** If the RPM sensor stops reporting valid data while
   Armed, the app gives a distinct "telemetry lost" warning, not the flameout
   alarm, so the pilot can tell the two apart.
8. **Telemetry window.** Shows current RPM, the state (OFF / DISARMED /
   ARMED / FLAMEOUT / NO TELEMETRY) and the active thresholds.

**Constraints.**

- Advisory only. The app never drives any output: it never calls
  `system.registerControl`, `system.setControl` or `system.setProperty`
  (constitution I). It only reads telemetry and switches and plays sound.
- It does not replace the ECU's own failsafe, shutdown or auto-restart logic,
  nor the pilot's own monitoring. The README must say so, and must say the
  app only works on turbines that send RPM telemetry.
- Low CPU: the loop does minimal work per call and never blocks; when Off it
  returns immediately.
- Standalone install: only `AG-FlmOt.lua` and its own `AG-FlmOt/` folder
  (alarm audio). No dependency on other apps in this repo. Any shared module
  from `src/Apps/lib/` must be listed in the plan, the install instructions
  and any release package.
- All settings persist per model with `system.pSave` / `system.pLoad`.

## Success criteria

- **SC-001.** In replayed or emulated flameout profiles, the alarm starts
  within (detection delay + 0.5 s) of RPM crossing below the threshold in
  100% of runs.
- **SC-002.** Zero alarms across a full normal flight profile: power-on,
  start sequence, idle, run-ups, throttle chops to idle, landing, and
  shutdown via the Cut switch.
- **SC-003.** Zero flameout alarms before the engine first reaches idle
  (start sequence, failed starts, cool-down).
- **SC-004.** Telemetry loss produces the "telemetry lost" warning, never the
  flameout alarm, in 100% of tests.
- **SC-005.** A pilot can enable the app for a model and configure sensor,
  switch and thresholds in under 3 minutes without any other manual.
- **SC-006.** The app never changes any transmitter output or setting
  (servo outputs and model file unchanged after a session with no user edits).
- **SC-007.** Copying only the app's files (plus any listed lib modules) to
  `/Apps` on a transmitter with no other AG- apps is enough to load and run.
- **SC-008.** Loop execution stays within Jeti's CPU budget with no "CPU
  limit" errors over a 30-minute emulator session.
- **SC-009.** On a model where monitoring is off (the default) or no RPM
  sensor is assigned, the app produces zero sounds or warnings in any
  scenario, including telemetry loss.
- **SC-010.** Enable state and sensor assignment are kept per model: enabling
  it on model A does not enable it on model B, and both settings survive a
  transmitter restart.
- **SC-011.** In replayed auto-restart profiles, the flameout alarm never
  clears while RPM is in the startup range, clears within (arming time +
  0.5 s) of RPM holding at or above idle, and fires no second alarm for the
  same flameout, in 100% of runs. A failed restart leaves the alarm active.
- **SC-012.** While the alarm is active, a "Flameout!" ×3 callout starts
  every 5 s (±0.3 s), the lock tone plays between callouts with no audible
  gap or overlap, and the cycle continues until acknowledge, relight or Cut,
  in 100% of tests.

## Edge cases

- **Turbine has no RPM sensor.** User leaves monitoring off; the app stays
  silent and shows OFF. Adding the app to such a model must never cause
  alarms.
- **User tries to enable with no sensor assigned.** Blocked, with an
  explanation.
- **No telemetry sensors listed at setup** (receiver off, or ECU not sending).
  Setup explains that telemetry must be received once before a sensor can be
  assigned.
- **Throttle chop from full to idle.** RPM falls fast but stays above the
  flameout threshold. No alarm. (Drives how low the threshold sits relative
  to idle.)
- **Brief telemetry dropout or a single bad sample.** Detection delay rides it
  out; no alarm.
- **Sensor reports 0 or invalid at power-on.** Stays Disarmed.
- **Failed start or hot start abort.** RPM never holds at idle for the arming
  time, so no alarm.
- **ECU auto-restart after flameout.** Starter cranking and ignition push RPM
  up through the startup range. The alarm keeps going (or stays
  acknowledged) until RPM holds at or above idle for the arming time, then
  clears and the app re-arms.
- **Auto-restart fails partway.** RPM rises into the startup range and falls
  back. No clear, no second alarm; the original flameout stays active.
- **Startup RPM peaks close to idle.** If the arming threshold is set below
  the highest startup/restart RPM, a restart could clear the alarm early.
  Setup warns when the idle value looks too low (e.g. below a learned idle)
  and the README explains the rule.
- **Idle RPM drifts slightly below the learned value** (temperature,
  altitude, fuel). The engine may not arm at idle and only arms on the first
  run-up. Acceptable for v1; a small tolerance setting is an open question.
- **Relight, Cut or acknowledge mid-callout or mid-beep.** All alarm sound
  stops at once; no further callouts.
- **Another app (e.g. Speed Gauge) is mid-announcement when the alarm
  fires, or when a callout is due.** The alarm must interrupt or pre-empt it,
  not queue behind it.
- **Audio file missing from `AG-FlmOt/`** (e.g. voice file not generated).
  The app falls back to built-in beeps (`system.playBeep`) plus the on-screen
  FLAMEOUT, and settings shows "voice files missing". A missing file must
  never silence the alarm.
- **Pilot flips to Cut during the alarm.** Alarm stops; state Disarmed.
- **Wrong sensor selected** (e.g. EGT instead of RPM). Setup warns if the
  selected sensor's unit isn't RPM-like, or if the value never changes.
- **Assigned sensor disappears** after an ECU swap, receiver rebind or model
  copy. App shows "sensor not found" and stays Disarmed instead of erroring;
  the user can re-assign or turn monitoring off.
- **Model copied from another model** (settings carried over). The copied
  sensor assignment may not exist on the new model's receiver; same handling
  as above.
- **Model loaded with no telemetry ever received.** App shows NO TELEMETRY,
  never alarms.
- **Thresholds set nonsensically** (flameout ≥ idle). Setup blocks it and
  explains.
- **Two engines.** Out of scope for v1; note as a possible extension (one
  instance per RPM sensor).

## Open questions / research (feed to `/speckit-clarify` and the plan)

1. **ECU RPM telemetry.** Which of Aaron's turbines/ECUs send RPM over Jeti EX
   (via Jeti Expander / ECU converter), and for those: units (rpm vs. x1000),
   decimals, update rate, and what the value does on flameout (falls smoothly,
   drops to 0, or goes invalid).
2. **Auto-restart profile.** Which of Aaron's ECUs auto-restart after a
   flameout, and what RPM they reach during cranking, ignition and fuel ramp
   before settling at idle. Use logs to confirm the startup range always
   stays clearly below idle, and to build the SC-011 replay tests.
3. **ECU status sensor.** Do Aaron's ECUs send a status / state sensor (e.g.
   "Running", "Flameout", "Restart", "Failsafe")? If so, could a state change
   confirm or trigger the alarm faster than RPM, or confirm a relight? Status
   codes differ per brand.
4. **Default thresholds.** Typical idle and max RPM for Aaron's engines, and
   what percentage of idle is safely below a throttle-chop dip. Gather RPM
   logs from real flights (Jeti log files) to set defaults and to build the
   replay tests for SC-001/SC-002. Decide whether the idle/arming threshold
   needs a small tolerance below the learned idle.
5. **Rate-of-drop detection.** Would RPM decay rate catch a flameout earlier
   than a fixed threshold without false alarms on chops? Evaluate on logs;
   keep threshold-only for v1 unless clearly better.
6. **Speed Gauge voice (resolved: Piper Amy).** Confirm which Amy model
   quality (low or medium) `tools/voice/` uses, and add per-phrase overrides
   (speed, pitch, EQ, compression) to the script so the urgent Flameout
   delivery doesn't change Speed Gauge's normal callouts.
7. **Audio playback API.** Confirm on firmware 6.x: `system.playFile`
   (priority / immediate mode so the alarm interrupts other announcements,
   including Speed Gauge), how to time the 5 s callout cycle from `loop()`
   with the lock tone in between (WAV loop stopped at callout time vs.
   `system.playBeep` bursts), how to stop playback instantly, and the
   supported WAV format. Also confirm `system.vibration` on the DS-24 II.
8. **Sensor validity.** Confirm the `valid` field returned by
   `system.getSensorByID` and how quickly it goes false after telemetry loss.
9. **Sensor identity across sessions.** Confirm that the sensor `id` + `param`
   pair from `system.getSensors()` is stable across power cycles so a saved
   assignment keeps working, and how it behaves after a receiver rebind or
   ECU/converter swap.
10. **Interaction with native alarms and voice output.** Avoid clashing with
    any existing native RPM alarm or the transmitter's own telemetry-loss
    announcement.
11. **Emulator testing.** Can JETI Studio replay a log or let us drive a fake
    RPM sensor to script the flameout, chop and auto-restart profiles? If
    not, plan a small test harness in `types/` or a debug mode that feeds
    synthetic RPM.
12. **Stub check.** Confirm `types/jeti.lua` declares `getSensors`,
    `getSensorByID`, `playBeep`, `playFile`, `vibration` and
    `registerTelemetry`; add any missing ones and note them in
    `docs/jeti-api-notes.md`.
